1. Executive Scientific Overview

L‑DREA (Deterministic Runtime Enforcement Architecture) evaluated here in its Gamma G‑0 instantiation is a reference monitor for action. It sits between an autonomous agent that proposes an action and the effector that would externalize it, and it decides — deterministically, per action, before any irreversible effect — whether that action may cross the boundary into the world.

The architecture rests on six load-bearing ideas. Each is measured by at least one experiment in this dashboard; none is asserted without evidence.

The six load-bearing concepts

ConceptDefinitionWhere it is measured
Deterministic Runtime GovernanceAuthorization is a pure function of the observed action context. The same input yields the same decision, always, and that decision is reconstructable from the evidence record alone.E1 E2 E3
Action BoundaryThe single, non-bypassable point at which a proposed action becomes an executed one. Nothing reaches the effector except through the boundary.E1 Pipeline

What L‑DREA is distinguished from

L‑DREA is not …Because
an ordinary classifierA classifier outputs a score and is judged by aggregate accuracy over a distribution. L‑DREA outputs an authorization decision per action and is judged by whether a single unauthorized externalization ever occurs. Its objective is bounded worst‑case behaviour, not expected‑case performance; scores are inputs to gates, never substitutes for them.
access controlAccess control asks who may invoke what, and answers once, at admission. L‑DREA asks whether this specific action, in this specific runtime state, at this instant may externalize — re‑evaluated for every action, with freshness and state‑consistency (TOCTOU) as first‑class predicates.
an AI safety filterA filter inspects content and is compensatory in practice: strong evidence of benignity can outweigh a weak warning signal. L‑DREA's aggregation is a maximum over deficits, so no amount of countervailing evidence can restore a permit once any predicate fails.
a monitoring systemMonitoring observes and reports after the fact. L‑DREA is an interlock: evidence is committed before actuation (commit‑before‑actuate), and a fault in the monitor causes denial rather than unobserved execution.
a policy engineA policy engine evaluates rules and typically fails open or falls back to a default when a rule is unavailable. L‑DREA treats an unavailable predicate as a deficit and absorbs into SAFE_STATE. Its outputs are additionally bound into a tamper‑evident hash chain, so a third party can re‑verify every decision without the engine (E2).

Goodhart resistance. A purely predicate-local guard can be defeated by an adversary who optimizes each predicate to its threshold while the action remains categorically impermissible. 10. Scientific Motivation

QuestionAnswer
Why does unauthorized execution matter?An unauthorized externalization is not a misclassification that can be corrected on the next batch. It is an irreversible effect in the world: a transfer settled, a message sent, a resource granted. The relevant figure of merit is therefore not average accuracy but whether the count is zero, and how tightly that zero is bounded — which is why every zero-event result in this dashboard is reported with its Wilson upper bound rather than as '0%'.
Why is fail-open unacceptable?A guard that permits when it cannot evaluate has inverted its own contract: it is most permissive exactly when it understands least. Under fault injection this is the difference between a system that degrades and one that silently stops being a guard. E8 injects missing, delayed, corrupted, conflicting and stale predicates; the required behaviour in every case is SAFE_STATE.
Why is authorization not prediction?A predictor is optimized for expected loss over a distribution and is permitted to be wrong on a minority of inputs. An authorization decision is a per-action commitment whose error is not averaged away. Scores may inform predicates; they may not replace them. This is why the aggregation is a maximum over deficits rather than a weighted sum over scores — and the negative control measures precisely what a weighted sum would leak.
Why is runtime governance necessary?Training-time and admission-time controls bind a model's intent and a caller's identity. Neither binds the action at the instant it would take effect, in the state it would take effect in. Freshness, context staleness and time-of-check/time-of-use consistency are only decidable at runtime, at the boundary.

Probe 2 — Corollary 2: the single-deficit counterfactual

InterpretationIf each adversarial row were reduced to an isolated single deficit (0.077 < tau=0.15), a compensatory gate would MASK the failure -> 492 COUNTERFACTUAL false permits; the non-compensatory max-aggregator (LLC) still denies all of them.

Why compensatory methods fail. Under a weighted sum, a single deficit contributes a bounded penalty (here 0.077) which the remaining concurring predicates can offset below the permit threshold τ = 0.15. The attack this admits is not exotic: an adversary need only ensure that exactly one predicate fails. Probe 2 constructs precisely that input and the compensatory rule permits all 492 of them.

Why the Law of Concurrence succeeds. ΓG = maxi(di) makes the penalty for a single deficit saturating rather than additive: one deficit sets Γ = 1 and no quantity of concurrence elsewhere reduces it. Soundness therefore does not depend on predicate weights, on τ, or on how many predicates happen to fail together in the observed data — which is exactly the dependence Probe 1 shows a compensatory rule to have, and Probe 2 shows to be fragile.

Scope of this claim. Probe 2 is a counterfactual over the same engine, not a separate dataset: it reduces each adversarial row to an isolated single deficit and re-adjudicates. It establishes that the compensatory rule's success in Probe 1 is an artifact of co-occurring deficits in the ULB mapping, not a property of compensatory aggregation. It does not claim that a real-world adversary against this corpus can produce single-deficit rows.

### I1 — Baseline recall below 1.0 is a property of the GENERATOR, not a runtime defect

- **Threat.** The intact stack permits 41 of 79 positives (`baseline_absolute_recall` = **0.481013**, `undetected_risk_rate` = **0.518987**, Wilson 95% [0.4105, 0.6257], `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_ablation.json`). Read naively this looks like a runtime failure.

## 3. Construct validity

### C1 — RIS is audit-weighted BY CONSTRUCTION

- **Threat.** The Runtime Integrity Score is a single scalar and could be mistaken for a security score.
- **Evidence (the definition, quoted from `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_ablation.json` → `runtime_integrity_score_definition`).** "mean of six [0,1] health indicators normalized so the intact baseline scores 1.0 (authz recall/baseline, revocation, evidence, ledger, hash-chain, replay). Four of six planes are audit/provenance, so RIS is audit-weighted by construction; the security axis is also reported separately."
- **Measured consequence.** Because 4 of the 6 health planes are provenance planes, removing an audit component moves RIS FARTHER than removing a security component, even though only the latter opens the authorization boundary. From `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_ablation.json`:
  - `remove_EQ`: RIS **0.3333**, `undetected_risk_rate` **0.519**, `evidence_completeness` **0.0**, verdict *AUDIT-DEGRADED (evidence/ledger integrity lost; authorization intact)*.
  - `remove_PE`: RIS **0.8333**, `undetected_risk_rate` **1.0**, `evidence_completeness` **1.0**, verdict *SECURITY-DEGRADED (authorization/enforcement weakened)*.
- **Mitigation actually implemented.** The security axis is reported SEPARATELY from RIS (`overall_runtime_verdict` distinguishes SECURITY-DEGRADED from AUDIT-DEGRADED from CRITICAL, and every interaction row carries an explicit `security_degradation` block). RIS is never used alone to rank component importance.
- **Residual risk (not mitigated).** RIS remains an unweighted mean of six planes; the 4:2 audit:security split is a modelling choice, not an empirical weighting.

### C2 — `ledger_integrity` has no sampling distribution

- **Threat.** `ledger_integrity` is reported with NO confidence interval, p-value or effect size, unlike every other metric. This looks like a gap in the statistics.
- **Evidence it is a construct property, not a gap.** `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_statistics.json` → `configs.baseline_full_LDREA.ledger_integrity` carries `deterministic` = **true** and an explicit `not_sampled_reason`: "ledger verification is a single deterministic structural check (chain + Merkle + hash recompute) over the whole ledger, not a sample of trials — it has no sampling distribution, so no CI, p-value or effect size is meaningful. The per-BLOCK chain-link proportion IS sampled and is reported as hash_chain_integrity."
- **Mitigation actually implemented.** The quantity that IS sampled — the per-block chain-link proportion — is reported separately as `hash_chain_integrity`, WITH intervals. So the ledger plane is not statistically unreported; it is reported through the estimator that has a sampling distribution.

### C3 — Metrics that cannot exist under a configuration are marked, not zero-filled

- **Threat.** After removing a component, some metrics have no referent (e.g. hash-chain integrity with no ledger). Silently coding them as 0.0 would fabricate a degradation.
- **Evidence.** `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_statistics.json` carries **38** explicit `undefined_reason` entries across 4 metrics (`hash_chain_integrity`×12, `ledger_integrity`×12, `predicate_coverage`×7, `predicate_pass_rate`×7), e.g.:
  - `remove_PE` / `predicate_pass_rate`: "predicate_engine disabled: no predicate is evaluated, so coverage is 0 and the pass rate is vacuously 1 (nothing can fail)"
- **Residual risk.** An undefined metric still contributes its plane's floor value to the RIS composite; that is intentional (a destroyed plane scores 0 health) but it means RIS and the per-metric statistics answer different questions.

### C4 — The mapped LAB corpus is oracle conformance, NOT fraud detection

- **Threat.** The mapped corpus `GAMMA_G0_CREDITCARD_FULL_mapped.csv` (284807 rows) could be mistaken for a fraud-detection benchmark.
- **Measured evidence of leakage (`label_leakage_audit.json`).** `label_leakage_present` = **true**; **5 of 12** examined engine inputs are perfectly disjoint across classes and are therefore each a 100%-accurate standalone classifier: `Gate_A3`, `Gate_A7`, `Lambda_G`, `HARM_RISK`, `ReasonCodes`.
- **Why.** "Every listed leaking input has value sets that are perfectly disjoint across the two classes over all rows. Each is therefore, alone, a 100%-accurate classifier on this corpus. They were produced from the label: gamma_map_raw.py writes 'Class == 1 -> HARM_RISK high, Gate_A3 & Gate_A7 & Lambda_G fail' and encodes the label verbatim into ReasonCodes."
- **Mitigation actually implemented.** E5b does NOT use that corpus. It runs the runtime stack on a label-blind observation stream (`Observation` has no label field) and on the three real datasets in the cross-dataset arm. Results derived from the mapped corpus are labelled ORACLE CONFORMANCE, and the audit lists what the leakage does NOT invalidate:
  - Replay integrity (E2) — independent of how predicates were produced.
  - Formal verification (E3) — a property of the decision model, not the corpus.
  - Predicate coverage (E9) — a synthetic suite over the frozen entry point.
  - Non-compensatory soundness — a property of the aggregation rule.

## 4. Statistical conclusion validity

### S1 — Multiple comparisons (the honest version)

- **Threat.** The design tests **19 configurations × 17 metrics**, producing **262 simultaneous hypothesis tests** against the baseline at `alpha` = **0.05** (`experiments/combined_ablation/combined_statistics.json`). **No family-wise or FDR correction is applied.** At alpha=0.05 one would expect on the order of 13 false positives by chance alone.
- **Measured mitigation (not a hand-wave).** 140 of 262 comparisons are flagged significant, and of those, **114 (81.43%) carry a `large` Cliff's delta** (large: 114, negligible: 20, medium: 4, small: 2). The conclusions this experiment actually draws (predicate-engine removal opens the boundary; evidence→ledger→hash-chain is a hard cascade) rest on saturated effects — FPR moving to 1.0, evidence completeness moving to 0.0 — not on marginal p-values near alpha. A Bonferroni correction (alpha/262 ≈ 1.91e-04) would not disturb them.
- **Residual risk (explicitly NOT mitigated).** Any INDIVIDUAL borderline p-value in `combined_statistics.json` should be treated as uncorrected and read with that in mind. The small/medium/negligible-effect significances (2 small, 4 medium, 20 negligible) are exactly the ones a correction would be most likely to remove.

### S2 — Bootstrap resolution bounds the achievable p-value

- **Threat.** Composite metrics use **300** bootstrap replicates (`n_bootstrap`, seed 12345, `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_statistics.json`). A percentile CI from 300 replicates cannot resolve tail probabilities finer than ~3.33e-03.
- **Mitigation.** Composite significance is read from whether the bootstrap difference CI excludes 0, not from a fine-grained p-value; proportional metrics additionally carry exact Wilson intervals and a two-proportion z-test, which do not depend on the replicate count.
- **Method, quoted.** "300 seeded bootstrap replicates recomputed from the per-decision data -> descriptives + percentile CI; significance vs baseline from the bootstrap difference CI excluding 0. Structural planes with no sampling variation (ledger verify) are held fixed, which is why RIS CIs are narrow."

### S3 — Small denominators on the security axis

- **Threat.** `undetected_risk_rate` and `blind_risk_detection_recall` are computed over the population AT RISK — the positives — of which the baseline evaluation window contains only **79** (vs 4421 negatives, 4500 evaluated, 1500 excluded as warm-up; `experiments/combined_ablation/combined_ablation.json`).
- **Measured consequence.** The baseline Wilson 95% CI on FPR is [0.4105, 0.6257] — a width of 0.215 around 0.519. Security-axis point estimates are therefore imprecise.
- **Mitigation actually implemented.** Every proportion is published WITH its Wilson interval rather than as a bare point, and the conclusions rely on effects that clear the interval by a wide margin (predicate-engine removal drives FPR to 1.0 at every threshold scale, not to a value inside the baseline CI).
- **Residual risk (not mitigated).** Enlarging the positive count would require a different generator prevalence; it was not done.

## 5. Ecological validity

### G1 — The fleet is processes on one host; there is no network

- **Threat.** The fleet arm could be read as distributed-systems evidence.
- **Evidence.** `production_evidence/fleet_summary.json` → `testbed_type` = **"multi-process (real OS processes, spawn), single host"** with `nodes` = **5**. The "nodes" are OS processes on a single machine.
- **Consequence, stated plainly.** No network transport is exercised, so packet loss, retry behaviour, partition tolerance and network-partition recovery are **not measured**. Their absence from the results is N/A, not a demonstration of perfect network reliability, and nothing in this repository should be read as such.

### G2 — Distributed clock skew / IEEE-1588 PTP is physically unmeasurable here

- **Threat.** A runtime that orders evidence by timestamp needs a clock-skew bound. None is provided.
- **Evidence that this is a physical impossibility on this testbed, not an omission (`production_evidence/runtime_clock_consistency_report.json` → `why_not_ptp`).** "PTP synchronises clocks across separate physical hosts against a grandmaster. On a single machine there is exactly one system clock, so there is nothing to synchronise. Distributed clock skew and PTP bounds require >=2 hosts and a PTP grandmaster; see FINAL_GAP_ANALYSIS.md."
- **What IS measured instead.** `clock_source` = `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, `timestamp_resolution_ns` = 41, `monotonic_consistency` = **true** over 20000 samples with 0 non-monotonic observations, wall-vs-monotonic drift -76.739 ppm.
- **Residual risk (NOT mitigated, and cannot be on this hardware).** Single-host monotonicity is a strictly weaker property than a bounded cross-host skew. Any multi-host deployment claim would require ≥2 physical hosts and a PTP grandmaster.

### G3 — The synthetic stream is not the world

- **Threat.** `evidence_level` of the whole E5b matrix is self-declared **"Synthetic Runtime"** (`experiments/combined_ablation/combined_ablation.json`). Its detection numbers describe a generator, not a production traffic distribution.
- **Mitigation actually implemented.** The cross-dataset arm re-runs the SAME 19-configuration matrix on three REAL corpora (`experiments/combined_ablation/cross_dataset_ablation.json` → `evidence_level` = **"Measured Runtime"**) precisely so that the structural conclusions do not depend on the synthetic generator.
- **Residual risk (not mitigated).** Latency, throughput and interaction-effect magnitudes are reported only from the synthetic arm.

## 6. Deployment validity

### D1 — Tier-S (pure software) only: no hardware claim is made

- **Threat.** Cryptographic and latency figures could be read as attestable hardware numbers.
- **Evidence (repository's own scope declaration).** The reference implementation is labelled **Tier-S** (`README.md`), and the repository explicitly lists Tier-H hardware (FPGA / SGX / HSM) as **Not Claimed** / out of scope, with no HSM, TPM or enclave anywhere in the tree (`FINAL_GAP_ANALYSIS.md`: *"No HSM, TPM or enclave"*; key custody is a published seed constant, not a key ceremony).
- **Consequence.** The measured latency is a software path with representative crypto and is **not comparable to HSM/FPGA figures**. No secure-boot, attestation or hardware-root-of-trust property is asserted.
- **Residual risk (NOT mitigated — deliberately out of scope).** A production deployment would need hardware key custody; nothing here evidences it.

### D2 — Watchdog stall DETECTION inside the per-config fleet is timing-sensitive

- **Threat.** A stall detector inside a small, saturated per-configuration fleet is a race: whether a stall is OBSERVED depends on scheduler timing, not only on whether the detector works. Attributing such timing noise to a component interaction would be an error.
- **Evidence that E5b does not do so.** The governance plane is **excluded from the ablation matrix**. `COMPONENT_REGISTRY.json` marks WD (Runtime Watchdog), FT (Fleet Telemetry), CK (Clock Consistency) and RD (Runtime Risk Detection) with `in_ablation_matrix` = **false**, and `paper_tables/table_combined_ablation_C.md` renders their impact cell as *"governance stage (not in ablation matrix)"*. Consequently the 13 interaction effects are computed ONLY over the 5 in-matrix components (PE, RV, EQ, LG, HC) — no timing-plane signal can enter the interaction analysis. Per-configuration governance blocks ARE present in the published run, but they are reported for context only and are not inputs to the interaction analysis.
- **Where the authoritative watchdog evidence lives.** `production_evidence/watchdog_scenarios_report.json` (experiment `E11b_watchdog_scenarios`, `evidence_level` = **Measured Runtime**, supervisor `runtime_fleet.Watchdog (real threading.Thread, per-worker liveness)`) drives the real watchdog through 6 distinct heartbeat scenarios: `all_scenarios_pass` = **true**, `total_false_triggers` = **0**. Method, quoted: "each scenario drives the real Watchdog through a distinct heartbeat pattern; detection/SAFE_STATE/recovery are read from the watchdog's own events, not asserted"
- **Residual risk.** Watchdog liveness is therefore evidenced by a dedicated scenario suite, NOT by the ablation matrix. Do not cite E5b for watchdog behaviour.

### D3 — What a deployer would still have to establish

- Multi-host operation (clock skew bound, network faults) — **no evidence in this repository** (see G1/G2; `production_evidence/fleet_summary.json` → `testbed_type` is single-host).
- Hardware key custody and attestation — **explicitly not claimed** (see D1).
- Production traffic distributions — the ablation matrix is `Synthetic Runtime`; only the structural conclusions were replicated on real corpora (see E1).

  Scientific motivation
    Auditability that cannot be exported is auditability that only the authors can exercise.

    was strengthened at the same time: it re-hashes every member from its bytes and confirms the
    recorded ledger digest still matches the live ledger. An empty or tampered bundle FAILS.
